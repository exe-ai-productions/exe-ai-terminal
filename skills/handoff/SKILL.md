Write a handover for whoever picks this work up next — possibly you, with
none of this conversation left in your head.

Go through the conversation from the beginning. Do not work from memory of
what it was roughly about; the details that matter are usually the ones that
felt too small to remember.

Collect, and leave out any heading that has nothing under it:

- **Context** — one or two sentences on what this was about.
- **Still open** — what is unfinished, waiting on an answer, or waiting on
  something outside this conversation.
- **Decisions** — what was settled and must not be reopened. Say why, briefly.
  A decision without its reason gets overturned by the next person who has a
  better-sounding idea.
- **Where things are** — files, folders, addresses, with full paths.
- **What went wrong** — dead ends, and what the lesson was. This is the part
  that saves the most time and is the first thing people leave out.
- **Rejected** — what was suggested and turned down, so nobody offers it again.

Write in the language of the conversation. Be specific: a number that was
measured beats an adjective, and a path beats a description of where
something roughly is.

Save it as a file and say where you put it.

What the user asked for in the same message wins over any of this. If they
said "two sentences", write two sentences — do not ask which of us to obey.
